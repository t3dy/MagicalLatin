Latin Quotation

Conjuro te, daemon, per omnes sanctos angelos et per virtutem omnipotentis Dei, ut compareas mihi sine mora et sine nocumento.
Translation: "I conjure you, demon, by all the holy angels and by the virtue of almighty God, that you appear to me without delay and without harm."

Source Information

Original Text: Sourceworks of Ceremonial Magic Dr Stephen Skinner David Rankine The Goetia of Dr Rudd libgen li

Cited by Scholar: Dr. Stephen Skinner, David Rankine

Work: Goetia of Dr. Rudd

Scholarly Commentary

⌘ Linguistic Analysis
Ecclesiastical Latin with magical terminology. The invocation of angels provides divine sanction for the binding. 'Daemon' (daemon) is the classical term for spirit or intelligence, not necessarily evil.
— Dr. Stephen Skinner, David Rankine
⌘ Magical Significance
This formula represents the core technique of grimoire magic: binding spirits through divine authority and verbal formulas. The conjuration works by asserting the magician's authority (delegated from God and angels) over the spirit.
— Dr. Stephen Skinner, David Rankine
⌘ Scholarly Interpretation
Skinner and Rankine analyze how Dr. Rudd modified older Goetia traditions to emphasize Christian theology and divine authority. The inclusion of angel names and holy formulas represents a Christianization of earlier pagan binding techniques.
— Dr. Stephen Skinner, David Rankine
