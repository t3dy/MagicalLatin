Latin Quotation

Imagines stellarum secundum virtutes et proprietates suas fabricantur, ut per illas virtutes celestiales inferiora elementa transmutari possint.
Translation: "Images of the stars are made according to their powers and properties, so that through those celestial powers inferior elements may be transformed."

Source Information

Original Text: Unknown Source

Cited by Scholar: Charles Burnett

Work: Picatrix (Ghāyat al-Hakīm)

Scholarly Commentary

⌘ Historical Context
Burnett demonstrates how Picatrix synthesized Arabic astrology with Neoplatonic philosophy to create comprehensive magical system. The image-making principle allowed medieval practitioners to operationalize abstract celestial correspondences. Picatrix became foundational for European grimoire tradition through this practical magical philosophy.
— Charles Burnett
⌘ Linguistic Analysis
Medieval Latin translation of Arabic astrological terminology. 'Secundum virtutes' (according to powers/virtues) uses classical Latin philosophical vocabulary adapted to magical context. 'Transmutari possint' (may be transformed) uses subjunctive passive, expressing possibility rather than inevitability. 'Inferiora elementa' employs Neoplatonic hierarchy terminology.
— Charles Burnett
⌘ Magical Significance
Core principle justifying talismanic magic: celestial bodies have real powers that operate through material images. An image crafted to match a star's nature and properties becomes a channel for that star's influence on earthly matter. This bridges theory and practice—understanding star properties enables practical manipulation of elements.
— Charles Burnett
