Latin Quotation

Universus hic mundus est animal viventis animatum, et habet animam quidem unam, sed omnia habet intra se, et totius animae portiones sunt partes mundi.
Translation: "This entire cosmos is an ensouled living creature, and it has a single soul, but contains all things within itself, and the parts of the whole soul are the parts of the cosmos."

Source Information

Original Text: Unknown Source

Cited by Scholar: D.P. Walker

Work: Enneads or related Neoplatonic text

Scholarly Commentary

⌘ Historical Context
Walker shows how Renaissance mages adopted Plotinus's World Soul doctrine to justify magical operations. The doctrine transformed magic from transgressive demon-binding to natural philosophy—by attuning to the cosmos's own life, the magus works with nature, not against it.
— D.P. Walker
⌘ Linguistic Analysis
Neoplatonic Latin adapted from Greek philosophical terminology. 'Animal viventis animatum' (ensouled living creature) creates redundancy for emphasis—emphasizing that the cosmos is not mechanically alive but spiritually ensouled. 'Portiones sunt partes' (portions are parts) plays on Latin word similarity to express the relationship between universal and particular souls.
— D.P. Walker
⌘ Magical Significance
If the cosmos is a living creature with a unified soul, then sympathetic magic operates by engaging this cosmic life principle. Human souls participate in the World Soul, making possible the deep correspondences that magical operations exploit. Operations work not through coercion but through attunement to cosmic living principle.
— D.P. Walker
