Latin Quotation

Amicitia et amore per virtutes celestiales viget. Anima mundi omnium rerum amore connuit et unit. Ideo qui amat sapientiam Deum amat.
Translation: "Friendship and love flourish through celestial powers. The World Soul unites all things through love and connection. Therefore whoever loves wisdom loves God."

Source Information

Original Text: Unknown Source

Cited by Scholar: D.P. Walker

Work: De Vita (Three Books on Life) or commentary on Plato's Symposium

Scholarly Commentary

⌘ Historical Context
Walker shows how Ficino's Neoplatonic love magic profoundly influenced Renaissance spirituality. By positioning love as the operating principle of cosmos, Ficino made magic and spirituality inseparable—the magus becomes a contemplative lover engaged with divine principle through sympathy rather than transgression.
— D.P. Walker
⌘ Linguistic Analysis
Renaissance Neoplatonic Latin synthesizing Platonic philosophy with Christian theology. 'Per virtutes celestiales' (through celestial powers) maintains Neoplatonic hierarchy. 'Anima mundi' (World Soul) translates Greek psyche cosmou. Present tense verbs ('viget', 'connuit') express timeless universal principle.
— D.P. Walker
⌘ Magical Significance
If the World Soul operates through love, then the magus's cultivation of love and affection becomes primary magical tool. This transforms magic from compulsion into resonance—the practitioner aligns their love with cosmic love. Talismans and operations work through sympathy rather than coercion.
— D.P. Walker
