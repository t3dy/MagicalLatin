Latin Quotation

Per hanc artem angelicam visions vobis videbuntur, et scientiam super omnes viros aquiretis, et linguas omnes intellegetis.
Translation: "Through this angelic art visions shall be shown to you, and you will acquire knowledge beyond all men, and you will understand all languages."

Source Information

Original Text: Unknown Source

Cited by Scholar: Claire Fanger

Work: Ars Notoria (The Notarial Art)

Scholarly Commentary

⌘ Historical Context
Fanger demonstrates how the Ars Notoria preserved high medieval visionary practice within magical tradition. The work synthesizes Scholastic theology with practical invocation, showing how medieval magic could be licit when framed as angelic collaboration rather than demonic coercion. This influenced later Protestant and Catholic magical philosophy.
— Claire Fanger
⌘ Linguistic Analysis
Medieval Latin with biblical allusions ('super omnes viros' echoes wisdom literature). Future passive constructions ('videbuntur', 'aquiretis', 'intellegetis') express certainty and inevitability. 'Hanc artem angelicam' (this angelic art) contrasts with goetic demonology—emphasizing licit, divinely-sanctioned magic.
— Claire Fanger
⌘ Magical Significance
Unlike grimoires that bind demons, the Ars Notoria seeks direct angelic collaboration. The promise of knowledge beyond human capability through divine revelation transforms magic from transgressive compulsion into theologically justified mystical experience. This justification allowed educated clerics to practice magic without inquisitorial danger.
— Claire Fanger
