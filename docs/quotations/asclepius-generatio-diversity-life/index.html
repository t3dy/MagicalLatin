Latin Quotation

Hoc instrumentum omnia generat in diversitate generationis, in sine-cessione vitae, in infatigabilitate operationis, in velocitate necessitatis, in sociabilitate elementorum, et in ordine earum quae fiunt.
Translation: "This instrument generates all things in the diversity of generation, in the unceasing flow of life, in the tirelessness of activity, in the rapidity of necessity, in the associability of elements, and in the order of things that come to be."

Scholarly Commentary

⌘ Historical Context
Copenhaver shows how the Asclepius Latinizes Greek Hermetic cosmology, replacing Greek dynamis (power) with Latin instrumentum (instrument/tool). The passage demonstrates Hermetic theology: the cosmos is neither merely mechanical nor purely divine, but rather the continuous instrument through which divinity generates reality. This philosophy justified Renaissance magic by positioning human art as participation in divine creativity.
— Brian P. Copenhaver
⌘ Linguistic Analysis
Classical Latin with Hermetic vocabulary. 'Diversitas generationis' (diversity of generation) employs abstract noun construction typical of Hermetic translation style. 'Sine-cessione' demonstrates medieval Latin neologism for Greek privative alpha + cessation. 'Infatigabilitas' and 'velocitas necessitatis' show Hermetic technical terminology. The participial phrase structure creates rolling cosmic action.
— Brian P. Copenhaver
⌘ Magical Significance
Core to Hermetic magical theory: the cosmos itself is a living instrument (organon) that generates reality through perpetual action. Practitioners working with natural magic rely on this principle—if the cosmos never ceases generating, then human art can align with and channel this generative force. Talismans and operations work by aligning human intention with cosmic generation-in-process.
— Brian P. Copenhaver
