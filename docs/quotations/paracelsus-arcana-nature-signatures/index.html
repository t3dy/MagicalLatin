Latin Quotation

Natura nihil creata sine signo. Omne quod est in rebus signature quaedam insculpta est, quae rem ipsam manifestat.
Translation: "Nature creates nothing without a sign. Everything in things has a certain signature inscribed in it, which manifests the thing itself."

Source Information

Original Text: Unknown Source

Cited by Scholar: William R. Newman

Work: De Ens Signatorum or Archidoxies

Scholarly Commentary

⌘ Historical Context
Newman demonstrates how Paracelsus's signature doctrine transformed magical epistemology: knowledge comes not from demons or books but from nature itself. This philosophy influenced both modern homeopathy and Enlightenment natural philosophy, showing how medieval magic evolved toward experimental science.
— William R. Newman
⌘ Linguistic Analysis
Renaissance Latin blending medieval scholastic and early modern empirical registers. 'Natura nihil creata sine signo' uses classical Latin word order for philosophical pronouncement. 'Signature' appears to be Paracelsus's own neologism from Latin signum. 'Manifestat' (reveals/makes plain) emphasizes the epistemological claim: signatures are knowable marks.
— William R. Newman
⌘ Magical Significance
If everything bears revelatory signs of its virtues, then the magus-physician's task is learning to read nature. This shifts magic from ritual and invocation toward empirical observation and natural philosophy. Signature doctrine enabled practitioners to claim medical/alchemical work was science, not magic.
— William R. Newman
