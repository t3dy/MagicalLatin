Latin Quotation

Conjuro te, daemon, per Deum vivum, per Deum sanctum, per Deum immortalem, ut mihi obedias et facias quod praecipio tibi.
Translation: "I conjure you, spirit, by the living God, by the holy God, by the immortal God, that you obey me and do what I command you."

Source Information

Original Text: Unknown Source

Cited by Scholar: Owen Davies

Work: Liber Juratus or related Solomonic grimoire

Scholarly Commentary

⌘ Historical Context
Davies shows how Goetic binding drew from medieval theology and penitential practice, adapting church language of divine command to magical purpose. The formula's power lies in its paradox: it uses God's authority to command what God would condemn, yet maintains theological coherence through the claim that binding demons is divine work.
— Owen Davies
⌘ Linguistic Analysis
Medieval Latin with classical grammatical precision: 'Conjuro te' (I bind/conjure you) uses first-person singular imperative with accusative direct object. Triple divine invocation (vivus, sanctus, immortalis) creates ritual power through repetition and escalation. Imperative 'obedias et facias' establishes absolute command structure.
— Owen Davies
⌘ Magical Significance
Central binding formula establishing the magician's authority over spirits. The triple invocation of divine attributes (living, holy, immortal) transfers divine power to the magician's command. The conjunction of obedience and action ('obedias et facias') makes this both a constraint and an authorization—the spirit must both submit and actively work.
— Owen Davies
