Latin Quotation

Mundus omnis in tres mundos dividitur: Elementarem, Coelestiem, et Intelligentem. Et homo secundum hunc ordinem componendus est, ut pars sit totius mundi.
Translation: "All the world is divided into three worlds: Elemental, Celestial, and Intelligible. And man must be composed according to this order, so that he may be a part of the whole world."

Source Information

Original Text: Unknown Source

Cited by Scholar: Brian P. Copenhaver

Work: De Occulta Philosophia Libri Tres (Three Books of Occult Philosophy), Book I

Scholarly Commentary

⌘ Historical Context
Copenhaver shows how Agrippa systematized Renaissance magical philosophy by integrating Neoplatonic metaphysics with practical correspondence magic. The three-world doctrine provided theoretical coherence to what had been scattered grimoire practices, enabling mages to see their work as natural philosophy operating through cosmic order.
— Brian P. Copenhaver
⌘ Linguistic Analysis
Renaissance Latin using scholastic philosophical terminology. The tripartite structure (Elementarem, Coelestiem, Intelligentem) orders reality hierarchically. 'Secundum hunc ordinem' (according to this order) suggests that magical effectiveness depends on maintaining cosmic structural correspondence. Present passive 'componendus est' emphasizes necessity.
— Brian P. Copenhaver
⌘ Magical Significance
If humans are microcosmic reflections of the three worlds, then operations on human will/intellect can affect corresponding celestial and elemental levels. This establishes the metaphysical foundation for Renaissance magic: the magus succeeds not through transgression but through right alignment with cosmic structure.
— Brian P. Copenhaver
