Latin Quotation

Ignis, Aer, Aqua, Terra: haec quattuor elementa omnium rerum compositionem constituunt, et in eorum mutatione omnis generatio et corruptio versatur.
Translation: "Fire, Air, Water, Earth: these four elements constitute the composition of all things, and in their change all generation and corruption revolve."

Source Information

Original Text: Unknown Source

Cited by Scholar: William R. Newman

Work: Alchemical treatise on elemental philosophy

Scholarly Commentary

⌘ Historical Context
Newman demonstrates how alchemists inherited and adapted Aristotelian elemental theory, creating experimental procedures based on philosophical principles. The alchemical innovation was treating elements as manipulable processes rather than philosophical abstractions, moving from theory to laboratory practice.
— William R. Newman
⌘ Linguistic Analysis
Classical Latin with scholastic philosophical structure. The nominative plural construction lists elements in traditional order (fire before water, air before earth in some systems). 'Constituunt' (constitute/make up) uses present tense to express timeless truth. The participial phrase 'in eorum mutatione' (in their change/mutation) emphasizes process over static state.
— William R. Newman
⌘ Magical Significance
If all material things are composed of elemental combinations, then the alchemist working with these elements can theoretically restructure any matter. This principle justifies the Great Work: transmutation is not miracle but understanding and manipulating nature's own processes. Success requires knowledge of how elements interact and transform.
— William R. Newman
