Latin Quotation

Universus hic mundus est unum animal infinitum, cuius anima est Deus, membraque omnes creaturae ab ipso fluxae.
Translation: "This entire world is one infinite animal, whose soul is God, and whose members are all creatures flowing from Him."

Source Information

Original Text: Elliot R Wolfson Abraham Abulafia Kabbalist and Prophet Hermeneutics Theosophy and Theurgy Cherub Press

Cited by Scholar: Elliot R. Wolfson

Work: Neoplatonic cosmology

Scholarly Commentary

⌘ Linguistic Analysis
Renaissance Latin synthesis of Neoplatonic (world soul) and Kabbalistic (emanation) thought. 'Fluxae' (flowing) emphasizes continuous emanation rather than creation as single act.
— Elliot R. Wolfson
⌘ Magical Significance
Provides the theoretical basis for understanding how magical operations affect the whole through parts. If all creation is interconnected through the world soul, then working with any part (herb, stone, angel) affects the whole.
— Elliot R. Wolfson
⌘ Scholarly Interpretation
Wolfson shows how Neoplatonic anima mundi concepts merged with Kabbalistic emanation theory to create a comprehensive mystical philosophy supporting magical practice as legitimate operation within divine reality.
— Elliot R. Wolfson
