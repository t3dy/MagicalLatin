Latin Quotation

Juro per Deum omnipotentem, Deum Creatorem, Deum Conservatorem, ut hanc artem unquam cuiquam, nisi idoneo et sapiente, revenem.
Translation: "I swear by God the Almighty, God the Creator, God the Sustainer, that I will never reveal this art to anyone except the worthy and wise."

Source Information

Original Text: Unknown Source

Cited by Scholar: Frank Klaassen

Work: Liber Juratus (Sworn Book of Honorius)

Scholarly Commentary

⌘ Historical Context
Klaassen shows how the Liber Juratus positioned magical knowledge as part of Christian theology rather than demonic transgression. The oath structure demonstrates medieval grimoire authors' sophisticated understanding of how sacralization (through divine names and binding oaths) could legitimize magical practice within Christian framework.
— Frank Klaassen
⌘ Linguistic Analysis
Medieval Latin with triadic divine invocation (Almighty, Creator, Sustainer) creating ritual power through repetition. 'Juro per...' (I swear by) uses legal formula language, treating magical oath as binding contract. 'Idoneo et sapiente' (worthy and wise) specifies the ethical prerequisites for knowledge transmission.
— Frank Klaassen
⌘ Magical Significance
The oath binds the practitioner not through demonological compulsion but through divine authority. By swearing before God, the magus places himself under sacred obligation—knowledge becomes inherently bound to ethical responsibility. This transformed magical practice from transgressive to theologically justified.
— Frank Klaassen
