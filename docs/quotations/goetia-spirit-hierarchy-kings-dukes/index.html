Latin Quotation

Sunt in hac operatione Reges et Principes, Duces et Marchioses, Magistratus et Milites, Praesides et Comites, qui per gradus varios distribuuntur.
Translation: "In this operation there are Kings and Princes, Dukes and Marquises, Magistrates and Knights, Presidents and Counts, who are distributed through various ranks."

Source Information

Original Text: Unknown Source

Cited by Scholar: Stephen Skinner and David Rankine

Work: The Goetia of Dr. Rudd

Scholarly Commentary

⌘ Historical Context
This passage shows how Goetic magic translated medieval feudal and Roman bureaucratic models into a spiritual hierarchy. The seventy-two spirits represent complete knowledge (like 72 divine names in Kabbalah)—their organization by rank suggests control through hierarchy rather than coercion. Skinner and Rankine's analysis reveals how grimoire authors understood power as structural: the magician succeeds by understanding the system, not by overwhelming force.
— Stephen Skinner and David Rankine
⌘ Linguistic Analysis
Medieval Latin using civic and military Roman hierarchy nomenclature translated into Renaissance grimoire terminology. 'Per gradus varios' (through various ranks) creates parallel to Roman administrative division. The seven titles mirror Roman administrative levels (King, Prince, Duke, Marquis, Magistrate, Knight, President, Count). Classical Latin syntax adapted for ceremonial precision.
— Stephen Skinner and David Rankine
⌘ Magical Significance
The Goetic hierarchy establishes the operational framework for spirit binding: spirits ranked by office hold different powers and must be addressed according to their station. A magician's authority derives from understanding and invoking correct protocol—kings command larger operations, while lower-ranked spirits have narrower domains. This explains why grimoire operations specify which rank of spirit should be invoked for particular goals.
— Stephen Skinner and David Rankine
