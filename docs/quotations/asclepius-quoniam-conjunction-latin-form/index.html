Latin Quotation

Quoniam deus omnia fecit ad similitudinem suam, et mundus est animatus et viventis est similis deo.
Translation: "Since God made all things in his likeness, and the cosmos is ensouled and is similar to God."

Source Information

Original Text: Unknown Source

Cited by Scholar: Brian P. Copenhaver

Work: Asclepius

Scholarly Commentary

⌘ Historical Context
Copenhaver's analysis of the Asclepius reveals how its Latin translator (or Apuleius himself) preserved Greek logical argument through carefully chosen conjunctions. Quoniam signals causal premises in metaphysical argumentation. The Asclepius's entire philosophical force depends on such conjunctions establishing cosmic resemblance as logically prior to magical practice.
— Brian P. Copenhaver
⌘ Linguistic Analysis
Quoniam as causal conjunction (Classical Latin for 'since/because') links premises in logical/theological argument. The repetition of 'similis' and anaphoric structure (deus... mundus) creates philosophical symmetry. Medieval variant for quoniam appears in some manuscripts; Copenhaver notes scribal variation across recensions (S1, S2, S3). The construction demonstrates how Latin maintained Greek logical flow in translation.
— Brian P. Copenhaver
⌘ Magical Significance
The quoniam proposition establishes the fundamental principle justifying magical operation: if cosmos and God share essential similarity, then knowing divine patterns (through Hermetic revelation) allows manipulation of cosmic patterns. The because-relationship (causal link) makes magic rationally defensible—it works not through miracle but through alignment with cosmic structure.
— Brian P. Copenhaver
