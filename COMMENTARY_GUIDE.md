# Commentary Writing Guide for Latin Quotations

## Purpose

Each quotation needs three complementary commentary sections that explain its linguistic form, magical significance, and scholarly context. This guide provides proven patterns from our exemplars and shows how to write each section consistently.

## The Three Commentary Fields

### 1. Linguistic Notes (2-4 sentences)
**What it explains:** Grammar, vocabulary, language forms, translation choices, historical language context

**Why it matters:** Readers unfamiliar with Latin should understand the specific language constructions and how they convey meaning

**Good pattern:**
- Identify the language (Classical Latin? Medieval? Translation from Greek/Arabic?)
- Name key grammatical forms (cases, tenses, rhetorical structures)
- Explain any unusual or technical vocabulary
- Note how the language choices matter to the meaning

#### Example from Emerald Tablet:
> "Latin translation of Arabic original (Balinas/Hermes Trismegistus). The bidirectional structure (superius/inferius) reflects the principle of correspondence. Medieval translation uses classical constructions. 'Miracula' connects magical operations to divine action."

**Pattern analysis:**
- Identifies source language (Arabic original, medieval Latin translation)
- Points out rhetorical structure (bidirectional = superius/inferius)
- Names key term impact ('Miracula' semantic weight)
- Shows how language embodies philosophy (correspondence principle)

#### Example from Goetia (Conjuro te, daemon):
> "Medieval Latin with formal imperative construction. The term 'daemon' (Greek daimon) refers to intelligence/spirit, not necessarily evil—a key distinction in medieval magic. Binding formula uses classical coercive syntax: 'Conjuro...per' (I conjure...by) is the standard structure for spirit compulsion in grimoire tradition."

**Pattern analysis:**
- Language historical context (Medieval)
- Etymological clarification (daemon ≠ demon)
- Formula pattern (Conjuro...per structure)
- Genre/tradition placement (grimoire binding convention)

### 2. Magical Significance (3-5 sentences)
**What it explains:** Why practitioners care about this quotation, what principle it establishes, how it's used operationally, what it justifies magically

**Why it matters:** Shows the reader how this quotation functions in magical theory and practice

**Good pattern:**
- State the principle (what core idea does this teach?)
- Explain how practitioners use it (application)
- Show what it enables or justifies (operational consequence)
- Connect to broader magical worldview

#### Example from Emerald Tablet:
> "Most fundamental principle in Western magic. Establishes the theoretical basis for sympathetic magic and correspondential operations. If all creation is interconnected (macrocosm/microcosm), then working with any part affects the whole. Justifies construction of talismans, sigils, and ritual correspondences aligned with celestial/elemental forces."

**Pattern analysis:**
- States principle: "Most fundamental principle...macrocosm/microcosm"
- Explains consequence: "working with any part affects the whole"
- Lists applications: "talismans, sigils, ritual correspondences"
- Shows scope: "celestial/elemental forces"

#### Example from Goetia:
> "Establishes the hierarchical structure of the spirit realm and the magician's position within it. The spirit hierarchy (Kings, Marquises, Dukes, etc.) must be honored through proper invocation and binding. Shows that spirits have fixed natures and attributions to planets/elements—the magician's power comes from understanding and properly invoking these correspondences, not from commanding spirits arbitrarily."

**Pattern analysis:**
- Principle: Hierarchy + correspondences = magician's power
- Operational: Proper invocation and binding methodology
- Shows what's justified: Working with assigned attributes rather than arbitrarily

### 3. Scholarly Interpretation (3-5 sentences)
**What it explains:** How modern scholars understand this quotation, its historical significance, what it reveals about the tradition, how it fits the scholar's argument

**Why it matters:** Places the quotation in academic context and shows its evidentiary weight

**Good pattern:**
- Name the scholar and their work
- State the scholar's core claim about this quotation
- Show how this supports larger historical/philosophical arguments
- Explain why the scholar highlighted this specific passage

#### Example from Emerald Tablet (Owen Davies):
> "Davies demonstrates how this principle enabled European mages to construct complex systems of correspondence linking celestial bodies, metals, stones, plants, and spiritual entities. The principle remains central to modern magical practice. Represents the synthesis of Platonic philosophy with mystical theology in magical tradition. Shows how Renaissance mages integrated classical learning with medieval Christian mysticism."

**Pattern analysis:**
- Scholar and claim: "Davies demonstrates how this principle enabled..."
- Historical significance: Shows synthesis of Platonic + Christian elements
- Continuity: "remains central to modern practice"
- Larger context: Integration of classical learning with medieval theology

#### Example from Neoplatonism (Elliot Wolfson):
> "Wolfson reads the world soul doctrine as evidence of Neoplatonic influence on medieval Jewish mysticism. This image of the infinite cosmos as a living organism reveals how Jewish philosophers adopted Plotinian cosmology while maintaining Torah interpretation. The 'universal soul' language parallels Enneads 4.4.32 but appears in a Jewish mystical context, showing the cultural synthesis that characterizes this period of intellectual exchange."

**Pattern analysis:**
- Scholar and work: "Wolfson reads...as evidence of"
- Specific historical claim: Neoplatonic influence on medieval Jewish thought
- Shows synthesis: Plotinian cosmology + Torah interpretation
- Textual linkage: Parallels Enneads (academic rigor)

## Writing Checklist

For each quotation commentary section:

### Linguistic Notes
- [ ] Language identified (Classical/Medieval/Translation)
- [ ] At least 1-2 grammatical or structural features named
- [ ] Key term(s) explained
- [ ] Relevance to meaning shown

### Magical Significance
- [ ] Core principle stated clearly
- [ ] How practitioners use it explained
- [ ] At least one specific application given
- [ ] Broader worldview connection made

### Scholarly Interpretation
- [ ] Scholar named and their work identified
- [ ] Their specific claim about THIS quotation stated
- [ ] Historical significance explained
- [ ] Why this passage matters in their argument shown

---

## Red Flags (Things to Avoid)

❌ **Generic language:** "This is important to magic" → ✅ "Establishes the hierarchical structure spirits must be properly invoked"

❌ **Unsourced claims:** "Scholars say..." → ✅ "Owen Davies argues in Grimoires (2009) that..."

❌ **Paraphrasing without context:** "It means things are connected" → ✅ "The bidirectional structure (superius/inferius) reflects correspondence—what affects one level affects all levels"

❌ **Vague implications:** "This influenced people" → ✅ "This principle enabled mages to construct talismans attuned to specific celestial forces"

❌ **Separating commentary from quotation:** Use the quotation's specific words in your analysis, don't just make general points

---

## Quality Metrics

A complete commentary section should:
- **Be accurate** — matches what the source text actually says
- **Be specific** — references exact terms and constructions, not generalities
- **Be relevant** — explains why THIS quotation matters, not why quotations matter in general
- **Be substantial** — 2-4 sentences minimum; shows real engagement with the material
- **Connect to the quotation** — reader can see the link between commentary and Latin text

## Writing Tips

1. **Read the full context** in the source PDF before writing commentary—commentary should reflect the complete passage, not isolated phrases

2. **Distinguish levels of knowledge:**
   - What the SOURCE TEXT says (linguistic/linguistic notes)
   - What PRACTITIONERS use it for (magical significance)
   - What SCHOLARS argue about it (scholarly interpretation)

3. **Use active voice:** "Davies demonstrates..." not "It is shown..."

4. **Name specifics:** "The Emerald Tablet establishes correspondence between levels of reality" not "an ancient text discusses connection"

5. **Show don't tell:** Instead of "this is important," show WHY it's important with examples

6. **Cross-check against exemplars:** When in doubt about tone or length, read how our 4 exemplars handled similar quotations
